# Reflection

Completing this laboratory activity gave me a much clearer picture of how the three major cloud providers compare, and how real-world business needs shape technology decisions rather than personal preference alone.

*1. Which cloud platform impressed you the most?*

Google Cloud Platform impressed me the most, mainly because of how tightly its services connect to AI and data analytics. Learning that Google built Kubernetes itself, and seeing how tools like BigQuery and Vertex AI fit together, made GCP feel like a platform designed with modern, data-driven workloads in mind.

*2. What similarities exist among AWS, Azure, and GCP?*

Despite their differences, all three providers follow a similar structural pattern: they organize infrastructure into regions and zones, offer a web-based management console alongside a CLI, provide virtual machines and object storage as core building blocks, and include an identity and access management system to control permissions. This made it easier to compare them side by side, since the underlying concepts transfer across platforms.

*3. How do business requirements influence cloud platform selection?*

This activity showed me that choosing a cloud provider isn't about picking the "best" one overall, but the one that fits a specific business's existing tools, budget, and technical goals. A university already using Microsoft products benefits most from Azure, while a startup chasing rapid, low-cost growth may lean toward GCP or AWS instead.

*4. What did you learn from acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant?*

Acting as a consultant taught me to think beyond technical specifications and consider constraints like budget, existing infrastructure, and growth expectations. Recommending a platform meant justifying the choice with real reasoning, not just listing features.

*5. How has your GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio improved after completing this mission?*

My portfolio now includes structured, well-organized documentation comparing multiple cloud providers, along with decision matrices and client-based recommendations. This adds a more professional, consultative dimension to my portfolio beyond basic technical notes.
